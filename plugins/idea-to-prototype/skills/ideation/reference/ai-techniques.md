# AI techniques in this session — reference card

The big idea: **what you get out of AI tracks the context you put in and how hard you push back.** AI is a thinking partner you challenge, not an answer machine.

## Core techniques

1. **Extending AI with skills & tools**
   An agent can be given reusable, packaged procedures (skills) and tools (web search, file writing). You're running one right now.

2. **Divergent generation + context-setting** *(idea sprint)*
   Prompt for *breadth* first ("give me 10 angles"), then converge. The richer the context you give, the better the output.

3. **AI as an adversarial thinking partner** *(the grilling)*
   Ask AI to interrogate and pressure-test *you*. Don't use it to confirm what you already believe — use it to find where you're vague or wrong.

4. **AI-assisted research + verification** *(landscape)*
   AI is fast at mapping a landscape — and confident even when it invents competitors or stats. Always verify its claims against real sources.

5. **Divergent ↔ convergent + constraint prompting** *(stretch → wedge)*
   Expand to the wildest version, then give the model hard constraints ("buildable by a beginner in a few hours") to get a realistic, narrow answer.

## Unlock techniques (going deeper)

6. **Premise surfacing** — get AI to make your hidden assumptions explicit, then attack the weakest.
7. **Contrarian synthesis** — push past "what everyone knows" to a genuine insight ("everyone assumes X, but…").
8. **Independent second opinion via subagents** — have a *separate* AI, blind to your conversation, cold-read your idea. One AI orchestrating another.
9. **Option generation** — make AI lay out distinct directions and choose deliberately, instead of anchoring on your first idea.
